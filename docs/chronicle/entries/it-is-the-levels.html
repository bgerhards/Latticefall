It is the levels

BALdecision 067

The owner, playing: “the placement of most turrets are barely able to hit the enemies on the field.” Nothing in this project could see that — the sim only asks whether a build wins. sim/coverage.py (LF-176) is the instrument built to look, and it agrees with the owner: the median gun has a target in range about a third of the time, roughly one in five placed emplacements does essentially nothing, and the geometry says why — range values short relative to lane length on most anchors, slots simply too far from the lane on five Act 1 anchors. No range or slot was changed to get this answer.

Game is slow at times. I think it's because the placement of most turrets are barely able to hit the enemies on the field. The pace is also very slow.the owner, playing

Nothing in this project could see that. sim/run.py only asks whether a build wins. tools/density.py reports what is on screen, not who can reach it. A board where every gun fires at the extreme edge of its range for a sliver of each pass grades identically, on both instruments, to a board sited well. Until today the owner's own eyes were the only instrument that existed for this.

sim/coverage.py (LF-176) is the missing measurement, built as two independent halves because the three candidate causes need different evidence. lane_coverage() is pure geometry — what fraction of the lane falls inside a tower's range from a given slot, by sampling the lane. No sim, no RNG, no build order. It is a ceiling: what the best possible siting of any available tower at that slot could achieve. InstrumentedSim drives the real, unmodified sim.engine.Sim and reconstructs per-emplacement telemetry from public state after each tick — whether a live targetable unit was in range, independent of cooldown, and how much damage each emplacement actually dealt. sim/engine.py is untouched: every class here is a subclass wrapping existing methods, so the parity digest never moved and no 1,440-run re-run was owed.

Proved at runtime, not argued

  • geometric hand-check: slot (2,3) r=3.2 -> 0.3368 (hand calc 0.335)
  • far-slot geometric check: slot (500, 500) -> 0.0
  • far-slot dynamic check: 0/1690 combat ticks in range, 0.0 damage, across a real 1-wave run
  • determinism: anchor-09/burst/hard identical across two runs (9 emplacements, 10712.0 total damage)
  • outcome parity: InstrumentedSim == Sim on every policy/difficulty across anchor-01, anchor-09, anchor-13, anchor-24

The last line is the one that matters: the instrument provably cannot change a grade. Two full 24-anchor runs came back byte-identical to each other on top of that.

The median gun has something in range about a third of the time. The long tail the owner predicted is real: roughly one in five placed emplacements in acts 1 and 2 does essentially nothing — 178 of 589 samples on anchor-10, 172 of 644 on anchor-13, 88 of 320 on anchor-02.

The geometric half separates the causes

Median lane-coverage ceiling — the best any tower could do from the best slot — is only about 40%. Double the ranges and it jumps to 97–100% on 17 of 24 anchors. That is verdict (b): the range values are short relative to lane length, not the slots.

Five Act 1 anchors are the other problem — verdict (a), geometry: anchor-01, 03, 04, 05 and 07 do not recover even at 2× range, so their slots are simply too far from the lane. anchor-02's p10 uptime is 0%. Only anchor-06 and anchor-20 come back sited adequately.

And it rules out the third candidate. LF-053 and LF-072 both record that the autobuild fills slots in priority order regardless of coverage, so the obvious suspicion was that what the owner watched play itself was never a sited board at all. The ceiling numbers rule that out as the main cause: if the best available slot can only cover 40% of the lane, no build policy can fix it. It is the levels.

The discipline worth naming

Not one range value was changed and not one slot was moved. Tuning before the instrument existed is exactly what decision 067 rejected when it deleted PRESSURE_FLOOR rather than raising it — a threshold fitted to today's data is not evidence. The instrument exists now, so the tuning can be done against it, and that is filed as LF-181 for BAL-04, which re-grades the whole campaign against the distribution, not the mean — the mean hides the tail, and the tail is the complaint.

Filed, not started: LF-177

The owner also asked for a fast-forward — the pace is slow to play at 1× and there is no way to speed it up. Deferred by the owner's own framing, but the trap is worth recording with it: a speed multiplier implemented by scaling delta is a rules change, and scripts/anchor_sim.gd and sim/engine.py are compared across 1,440 runs on two platforms where float64 accumulation over a different number of differently-sized steps does not reproduce. The safe shape, if this is ever picked up, is the same fixed tick run more times per rendered frame — never a larger dt.
