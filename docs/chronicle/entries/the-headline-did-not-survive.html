The headline did not survive its own follow-up

BAL

‘largest-hole-closes’ published Threshold Surge flipping 9 of 72 anchor-by-difficulty cells from loss to win, with its own caveat attached: charge accrual was unmodelled, and the next regrade could not start until it was. LF-163 modelled it. The claim does not reproduce — zero cells flip — and the reason is not that boards fail to earn charge. This is that correction, appended rather than edited in, the way the rule says it has to be.

This entry corrects ‘The largest hole in the project closes: a grade that presses buttons’ (largest-hole-closes, commit 4daeb7c, #89), published earlier the same day. That entry is not edited — nothing in it is wrong about what it measured, and the chronicle does not rewrite a past entry when a later one overturns it. It gets a new one, linked back, the same way docs/DECISIONS.md never edits a settled decision. This is that entry.

The published finding was precise: Threshold Surge, fired on a deterministic schedule with no charge, cooldown or cost modelled, flipped 9 of 72 anchor-by-difficulty cells from loss to win — sweeping all three difficulties on both anchor-12 and anchor-13 — and was 0 worse across all 72. The entry declined to read that as mispricing. It named the gap plainly — charge accrual did not exist in sim/engine.py at all — and it named the follow-up: LF-163, model charge, re-run those nine cells, and block BAL-04's regrade on it. LF-163 is now closed.

The first finding reframes the task

The gap was never in the rules. scripts/abilities.gd's AbilityState already tracked Surge's charge in full — charge_max, charge_per_leak_cost, ready(), began() — and scripts/anchor_view.gd's _charge_surge() was already wired to AnchorSim's unit_killed signal, live, on every kill. That code sits deliberately outside anchor_sim.gd itself, on the same precedent decision 033 already set for sell(). What was unmodelled was sim/engine.py's and scripts/test/parity.gd's own scheduled-dispatch layer — the machinery BAL-01 built to press buttons on a fixed clock. Both files carried the same admission in their own words: Surge "has no charge/cooldown model in this file at all." The rule itself was never in question. scripts/anchor_sim.gd has a zero diff.

The port is the existing rule, not a new one: charge += leak_cost * charge_per_leak_cost on every kill from either _damage() or Surge's own _fire_surge(), clamped at charge_max, ready at >= charge_max, reset to zero on an actual cast. A scheduled cast whose time arrives before a full charge has accrued is still consumed off the schedule but does nothing — mirroring anchor_view.gd's activate_ability() returning nothing and playing a deny cue on a press that isn't ready yet.

Proved red before it was trusted

A gate that silently no-oped would have reproduced the original 9-of-72 result exactly and looked like confirmation rather than correction. It did not: anchor-12, brutal, surge-on-peak — 12 scheduled attempts, 3 actually fired, 9 denied for insufficient charge, with charge reaching the full 100 only at t=135s, 295s and 415s of the run.

All 72 cells were then regraded charge-gated against ungated, run twice — byte-identical both times — and cross-checked against the sim.run CLI with 0 mismatches. Zero cells flip.

Why zero, and the real finding underneath it

Not because boards fail to earn charge — they don't. Anchor-12 at brutal difficulty earns three full charges from ordinary combat across a roughly 470-second run, which is exactly what the original entry's own design note predicted when it estimated the wins needed five to fourteen casts rather than one. The actual reason is that **surge-on-peak is not the sole winner on any of the 72 cells under today's ruleset, gated or not.** Gating drops it out of 7 cells' winner lists — anchor-11 standard, and anchor-12 and anchor-13 at all three difficulties, exactly where the original entry flagged its own concern, so the location of the worry was real — but every one of those 7 cells is already won independently by a conventional build with no ability involved at all: mortar-core, burst, flak-screen or intel-first, depending on the anchor. The loss-without-Surge premise the whole “flips 9” headline rested on is gone. “0 worse across all 72” still holds.

Two loose ends, on the record rather than buried in a backlog item

Nobody knows why the winner lists moved between the two measurements. The obvious explanation is ruled out: BAL-01's own report says the four capped-core policies — mortar-core among them — were already in its 16-policy baseline when it ran, so their arrival is not what changed. Candidates not yet investigated: the balance fixes LF-098, LF-129, LF-130 and LF-136 landed in between, or a content edit to anchor-12 or anchor-13 itself. Unresolved, filed as part of LF-172.

And three of the nine cells were never written down. BAL-01 said nine. Only six are named anywhere — anchor-12 and anchor-13 at three difficulties each — across the issue spec, the original chronicle entry, docs/DECISIONS.md and the backlog. The other three cannot be checked, because nobody recorded which cells they were. That is a finding about how a measurement gets written down here, not just about Surge.

The angle

This is a good day for the method. A result was published with its own caveat attached and a named follow-up; the follow-up was done; the result did not survive; the correction is a new entry rather than a quiet edit to the old one. That loop working — catching its own headline rather than letting it stand uncontested — is the entire point of keeping the chronicle and the backlog this way. The uncomfortable half is just as real: a measurement can rot in a single day of otherwise good work, and this one did, and three of its nine data points were never recorded well enough to even check.

The practical upshot: BAL-04 is unblocked. No grade in the current 72-cell table depends on Surge being available on demand. But it inherits no evidence either way about whether the 130-damage, 35%-falloff numbers are correctly tuned — nothing in this regrade exercised that question, because nothing in the current winner lists depends on the ability at all. “The grader has no evidence either way, because nothing depends on the ability” is what BAL-04 actually gets handed. That is a different, and more useful, thing to hand it than either “trust it” or “don't.”
