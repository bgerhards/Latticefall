Measuring the fifth non-negotiable, for the first time

PRC

CLAUDE.md's fifth non-negotiable — loudness-match audio, never peak-normalize — had never been measured by anything in the gate. The only audio check, check_sfx_reproducible, verifies determinism, not loudness; a synthesis change that reproduces byte-identical but drifts the whole bank's perceived level would have passed every check the project has ever run. PRC-18's testing audit found the gap. tools/audio/loudness.py closes it with a real ITU-R BS.1770-4 / EBU R128 gated integrated LUFS implementation, validated three independent ways and proved red on real assets before being trusted — and it ships with its own blind spot named rather than hidden.

CLAUDE.md states five non-negotiables for this project. The fifth reads: "Loudness-match audio, never peak-normalize — peak normalization is why programmer audio sounds flat." It has sat in that file since early in the project. Nothing in the gate ever checked it. The only audio check that exists, check_sfx_reproducible, verifies exactly one thing — that re-synthesizing ui_confirm.wav reproduces the committed bytes — which is a determinism check, not a loudness one. A synthesis change that is perfectly deterministic but drifts the whole bank's perceived loudness, the precise failure the non-negotiable names, would have passed every check this project has ever run, for the project's entire life. PRC-18's testing audit is what found the gap, rather than anyone noticing it was missing.

A real implementation, not a proxy

tools/audio/loudness.py is a real ITU-R BS.1770-4 / EBU R128 gated integrated LUFS measurement — K-weighting (a high shelf plus a high-pass), 400 ms blocks at 75% overlap, the −70 LUFS absolute gate, the −10 LU relative gate. Two details worth recording because they are the kind of thing that is easy to get quietly wrong: the K-weighting filter coefficients are derived from each file's own sample rate, not the standard's published 48 kHz table, because the SFX bank is 44.1 kHz and the music is 48 kHz — resampling to reuse the published numbers would have been exactly the kind of precision the measurement doesn't have. And a first pass took minutes to run, because raw rfft on prime-length audio files is pathological; padding to the next power of two fixed it. SFX now measures in about 2 seconds across 86 files; music takes about 85 seconds across 14, because full-length stereo FFTs over several-minute tracks dominate the cost — which is also why the tool is not one check but two, split by tier.

Validated three ways before being trusted

An unvalidated LUFS number that looks authoritative is worse than an honest peak/RMS proxy, so this was checked three independent ways rather than assumed correct because the formula was copied faithfully. First, calibration: a 0 dBFS 997 Hz sine measures −3.052 LUFS at both 44.1 and 48 kHz, against −3.01 LUFS, which is a BS.1770 fact independent of any implementation. Second, gain linearity: shifting that same signal by ±6.02 dB moves the measurement by ±6.02 LUFS to within 1e-4. Third, and the one that matters most because it is the only one checked against a second, independently-written implementation: assets/audio/music_manifest.json's target_lufs field is what ffmpeg's loudnorm filter — already in this repo, driving tools/audio/ingest_music.py — two-pass-normalized every one of the 14 shipped music tracks to. Measuring the shipped files with this tool and comparing against that target is a cross-check between two separately-written BS.1770 implementations, not a self-check. The result: median delta −0.03 LU, mean −0.00 LU, worst 0.58 LU, across all 14 tracks. Two independent implementations of the same standard, agreeing to within a rounding error.

Bands anchored to the bank, not to a broadcast target

The pass/fail band for each category is mean ± 3 sample-stdev of the *current accepted bank*, measured at commit 048dcfd — never a broadcast target like −14 or −23 LUFS. Decision 011 already records what happens on this project when a metric gets tuned against itself; anchoring to a fixed external number would have meant either picking one that happened to already fit, or immediately failing the whole bank. SFX and music are banded separately, because a UI hover tick and a three-minute music bed are not comparable material and gating them together produces a check that is either useless or permanently red: SFX bands to (−34.05, −10.87) LUFS, mean −22.46, σ 3.86, n=86; music bands to (−26.45, −11.69) LUFS, mean −19.07, σ 2.46, n=14.

Proved red before being trusted green

A check that has never failed has not been proven to work — it has only been proven not to have failed yet. So before this was trusted, it was run against real committed assets, copied to scratch and deliberately shifted: shield_break.wav at +6 dB measured −8.89 LUFS, FAIL. threshold_idle.wav at −6 dB measured −36.77 LUFS, FAIL. A1-FIN_eleven_years_of_nothing.ogg at +6 dB measured −11.03 LUFS, FAIL. SYS-DBF_debrief.ogg at −6 dB measured −30.02 LUFS, FAIL. Run against the untouched, unmodified bank: 100 of 100 files pass, exit 0.

The honest half: what a band anchored to a bank's own spread cannot see

The SFX band is 23.2 LU wide, and that width is not slack in the measurement — it is because the cues in this bank are deliberately not loudness-matched to each other. A hover tick and a heavy footfall are not supposed to be equally loud; that is mix balance, not drift. The consequence was measured rather than left as a theoretical caveat: ui_confirm.wav at +6 dB still passes. A mid-band cue can drift a full six decibels and this check will not notice, because six decibels does not carry it outside the bank's own historical spread.

Underneath that sits a structural limit that no band width can fix: a per-file band anchored to a bank's own mean and spread can never catch a uniform bank-wide drift. Shift every cue in the bank by the same N dB and the mean moves, the standard deviation moves, and the band — computed from that same shifted data — moves with it. The check would stay green through the exact failure the fifth non-negotiable exists to prevent: a whole bank drifting hot or flat together. This is filed as LF-168, and the instruction recorded there is deliberately not "narrow the band" — narrowing it would start failing cues the sound design intends to be quiet, repeating the mistake decision 011 already warns against. What it needs instead is a second signal: the bank's mean LUFS tracked against a frozen historical baseline that does not move when the bank does.

So the honest summary is two-sided. A stated non-negotiable went from zero measurement, for the entire life of this project, to a validated implementation that catches real per-file drift at the edges of what the sound design allows — that is a genuine gain. And the same tool ships with a named, proven blind spot at its center and a structural one underneath that no amount of tightening fixes. Both halves belong in the record; a journal that kept only the first would be exactly the kind of quiet overstatement this tool was built to stop happening to loudness.

Not wired in yet, and that is deliberate

tools/check.py is not touched by this change. That file was owned by another workstream in flight the same session this landed, so wiring the two checks in was left for that workstream to pick up rather than risked as a collision. The registration is recorded in the commit body rather than left to be re-derived: Check("sfx loudness", 2, check_sfx_loudness) and Check("music loudness", 4, check_music_loudness), split across tiers because the SFX half costs about 2 seconds and the music half alone, at roughly 85 seconds, would exceed tier 3's entire current budget on its own.
