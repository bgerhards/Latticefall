Both mechanical ways to catch a stale number in prose are wrong, so the line declares which number it quotes

BALPRCdecision 092

Decision 092. I went looking for one thing and found another. LF-262 says Act I is a reveal gate — across anchors 02–08 a build containing a scan relay won 136 of 246 runs and a build without one won 0 of 195 — and my framing of that in the backlog was "the first hour of the game has one real decision and the player is not told". The second half was wrong, and checking it first is what turned up a different defect: anchor-04's brief tells the player the shield wall costs "forty of those ninety-four" megawatts. It draws 26, and has since LF-032 lowered it. The interesting part is what happened next: both obvious mechanical checks for this class of error were tried against the real dialog corpus and both fail, in opposite directions, so the fix is that a line now declares which data figure it reads aloud.

The previous entry, the-same-weight-in-fewer-bodies, closed out a content slice of BAL-04. This one is much smaller and started as a question rather than a task: LF-262 had just recorded that Act I is decided by one binary — across anchors 02–08, a build containing a scan relay won 136 of 246 runs and a build without one won 0 of 195, because the Warden's air unit cannot be targeted at all until something reveals it. The backlog item's justification line read "the first hour of the game has one real decision in it and the player is not told".

The second half of that was wrong, and it was checked before anything was built on it. anchor-02's brief has Okonkwo say "Motes. They run cold and they don't reflect — your guns will not see them without a relay lit", at exactly the anchor where the scan relay unlocks, and Vasquez answers it with the price. The gate is taught, by the right character, at the right moment. What remains open in LF-262 is purely the design question the item already states — whether a mandatory 8 MW relay should be a tax or a trade — and nothing about it needed writing.

The sweep that settled it found a different defect

anchor-04's brief tells the player: "Shield wall's signed off — forty of those ninety-four if you raise it", and Vasquez answers "Forty. For a screen that doesn't shoot." The shield wall draws 26. It drew 40 until LF-032 lowered it as overpriced, and the prose was never updated — a figure the player is read before the level, wrong by 14 MW, for the whole life of the project.

dialog capacity is a check that exists precisely because tuning moves numbers that prose quotes, and it did not catch this, because it asks exactly one question: does the brief state its own capacity_mw. It does. Ninety-four is correct.

Both obvious checks are wrong, in opposite directions

This is what the entry is actually about. Two mechanical designs were tried against the real dialog corpus before either was implemented, and both fail:

  • A regex over "<number> megawatts" misses this exact bug. "forty" carries no unit word at all — the unit is implied by the "ninety-four" it is compared against. The existing _spoken_numbers() helper, which anchors on the unit word, returns {94} for that line and never sees the 40.
  • "Every spoken number must be a live data figure" false-positives immediately. anchor-06 says the ion lance "reaches across four lanes". anchor-08 says "the ring is drawing about nine megawatts that isn't going anywhere I can find", and anchor-17 says "the bus reading falls about six megawatts a wave and I cannot find where it goes".

Those last two are deliberately unexplained. They are the act's mystery. A check that demands they resolve to a data figure is a check that demands the story stop being a story — and that is the argument against inferring the dependency at all, not a tuning problem in the regex.

So the line declares what it quotes

A dialog line may now carry quotes, a list of dotted paths into data/towers.json or data/enemies.json — shield-wall.draw_mw, restorer.effect.value, reach-sapper.drains_mw. The new tier-1 check dialog figures resolves each path and asserts the live value is among the numbers the line actually says, spelled or in digits. Ten figures are declared across seven anchors' dialog files today. The writer names the dependency; the check does the arithmetic.

The limitation, stated here rather than discovered later: it is opt-in, so it cannot prove a line should have been annotated. A future brief that quotes a draw without declaring it is invisible to this check exactly as anchor-04 was. What it does guarantee is that a declared figure can never be silently invalidated by a tuning change — which is the failure that actually happened, twice now if you count decision 090's stale note on the same emplacement.

Proved red four ways before being trusted green

injected faultresultwhy it is in the list
a tuning change moves the value, 26 → 28FAILthe real failure mode, reproduced
a typo'd emplacement idFAIL"names no emplacement or unit"
a path landing on an object, not a numberFAILa silently-unresolvable path is a silently-absent check
a full rewording that keeps the figureokthe design claim, not a formality

The last row is the one that matters. The check has to survive a writer moving the figure anywhere in the sentence, or the next narrative pass will start working around it — rewriting the line to "Raising it costs twenty-six" stays green. The tree was asserted byte-identical after all four injections.

One real bug in the checker, found by running it. The first _all_spoken_numbers() accumulated across sentence boundaries: "Anchor fourteen. Ninety-four megawatts" came back as 108, because splitting on non-letters made "fourteen" and "ninety" adjacent tokens in one run. It now chunks on anything that is not a letter, a space or an ASCII hyphen, so a full stop or an em dash ends a number and a hyphen does not. Covered afterwards by "a hundred and ninety" → 190, "two hundred and ninety" → 290, and "forty-four megawatts back for ten drawn" → 44 and 10.

A diff-discipline failure worth recording

The first pass at annotating the seven dialog files wrote them back with json.dumps(indent=2) and produced 858 insertions of pure formatting churn against this project's compact house style, for eleven lines of actual content. Rebuilt from git show HEAD: with surgical inserts instead: 11 insertions, 2 deletions. Same content, a diff 78 times smaller — and the difference between a change a reader can check at a glance and one nobody will read.

The prose fix keeps the beat. "forty of those ninety-four" became "twenty-six of those ninety-four", and Vasquez's echo "Forty." became "Twenty-six." 26 of 94 is still over a quarter of the bus for a screen that does not shoot, which is the objection the exchange exists to make, so the correction cost the scene nothing.

And the cost of shipping it, filed as LF-273. This is a data change under data/, so the parity digest forced a full 1,512-run comparison on both platforms — for seven dialog files and a schema that neither rule engine reads. The digest hashes 52 such files (25 dialog, 21 schema, 6 scenario) against 27 genuine rules inputs. That is roughly a 35-minute tax on changing one word of a mission brief, and it is filed rather than fixed here.

Gate at tier 3: 43 passed, 0 failed. rules parity 1512 runs identical on Linux, digest 234f711c8e46, freshly verified, and 1512 identical across 8 shards on Windows. music loudness at tier 4 was not run, because no audio file changed. The check counts in check.py's own docstring moved with it — tier 1 from 19 to 20, tier 2 to 29, tier 3 to 43, tier 4 to 46 — and tier counts asserts them against the registry, so they cannot drift quietly.
