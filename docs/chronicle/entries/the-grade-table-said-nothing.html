The derivation was right. The prediction was wrong. The grade table said nothing.

BALdecision 082

BAL-04 / LF-187, decision 082. range in data/towers.json is an absolute distance in tiles, tuned against 18×15 boards whose lanes run 24–51; decision 073 moved the board target to 48×48, where the generated lanes run 68–80. tools/range_derive.py holds own-lane coverage fixed and bisects for the range that recovers it — a per-emplacement ladder from 1.36× to 1.86×, with the mortar landing on 13.0 exactly. Then the honest check. The confident prediction that applying the ladder now would gut the shipped campaign was measured, and it is wrong: all 24 anchors still grade ok. What the grade table cannot see is that the top difficulty dissolves.

range in data/towers.json is an absolute distance in tiles. It was tuned against 18×15 boards whose lanes run 24 to 51 tiles, and decision 073 then moved the board target to 48×48 — where the generated board's lanes run 68 to 80. A constant absolute range covers a shrinking share of a wave's route as the board grows, and it shrinks silently: nothing anywhere in the campaign goes red about it. This is the range half of BAL-04 and LF-187, and it was the top item on the priority list because every theatre-scale board is behind it.

The invariant is own-lane coverage, and the two better metrics could not be used

tools/range_derive.py (new, 561 lines) holds exactly one quantity fixed — own-lane coverage, the share of one lane's length a slot can reach, taken as the median over a board's authored slots — and bisects for the range at which a generated 48² board recovers what a shipped act-3 slot has today. Two coverage metrics already exist in sim/coverage.py and neither one is the right instrument here, which is worth saying because both were considered first:

  • lane_coverage() is length-weighted across every lane, so it divides a well-sited gun's score by the lane count and reads four lanes as a range problem. A board with more lanes would ask for more range purely for having more lanes.
  • presence_coverage() is much the better predictor of real uptime — rank correlation +0.748 against +0.520 (LF-217) — but it is measured from a live run on the board's own slots. It does not exist until the board has been graded, and this solve has to run before there is any content to grade. So the geometry is the derivation and the grader is the check, in that order.

Plus the four upgrade.range overrides, solved the same way — 4.4→7.1, 5.4→9.0, 6.8→12.3, 8.0→15.2 — because an upgrade whose range does not scale with its base becomes a downgrade.

The mortar lands on 13.0 exactly. A previous session posted a target of about 13 tiles to issue 17 by a completely different route, and that number is the whole reason this pass had a target at all. Reproducing it from lane geometry turns it from an input into a check.

Two things a single multiplier would have got wrong

The ladder does not scale uniformly. 1.36× at the short end against 1.86× at the long: a short range on a winding lane sits on the steep part of the coverage curve and a long one does not, so one factor applied to every row is wrong in both directions at once.

The support emplacements belong in the derivation, and decision 074 left them out. shield-wall (slow), scan-relay (reveal) and anchor-damper (damp) have their range tested against unit positions by Sim._covered_by(), with the same squared-distance compare the firing loop uses — they are lane-coverage quantities exactly as much as a weapon's range is. Holding them fixed at 48² would have silently halved the Act II damper decision that decision 027 exists to create.

One field that reads as tuning and is not

restorer.range is never read by either rules implementation. Sim.capacity_now() (sim/engine.py:608) adds effect_value for every online restorer with no distance test at all, and scripts/anchor_sim.gd:383 mirrors it. That is a measurement rather than a reading of the code: anchor-20 was graded with the restorer's range at 1.0 and again at 40.0 and by_difficulty came back identical field for field — and the same assertion returns False when pointed at pulse-turret, so it discriminates rather than being vacuously true. The restorer is therefore excluded from the ladder, because scaling nothing is nothing. It is the only emplacement in the game whose siting is free. LF-242 carries the real question, which is whether it should be.

What the ladder buys on the board it was solved for

On the generated 48² board at the authored ranges, brutal is a single-solution level — 1 winning build out of 16 tried — and all three difficulties die on wave 8. At the derived ranges it is 4 of 16 at all three, it grades ok, and the death wave separates for the first time: 9 / 9 / 8.

The prediction was made confidently, checked, and was wrong

This is the part of the session worth keeping. Before anything had been measured, the position stated plainly in this session was that applying the ladder now would gut the current game — a 13-tile mortar on a 41-tile lane is absurd on its face, so the shipped campaign would fall over. --shipped-impact was written to check that claim rather than to assert it.

It is wrong. All 24 shipped anchors grade 24/24 ok at the derived ranges, completely unchanged. Not one of them notices.

What actually happens is a failure the grade table has no column for: the top difficulty dissolves. On brutal the share of tried builds that win goes 25% → 43%; the median anchor's distinct winning builds goes 3 → 6; anchor-02, 09 and 11 go from 2 winning builds to 8, 7 and 8. And not one anchor trips sim/run.py's difficulty-is-not-biting rule, because that rule fires only when every distinct build clears — the closest any anchor came was anchor-14 on standard, at 13 of 14.

So this is the second independent proof that the grade table is not a valid selector, after decision 081 found board quality and grade count anti-correlated across five lattice orderings. There a green grade could not choose the right answer; here a green grade cannot see the wrong one. LF-243 carries the candidate replacement — win share per difficulty, required to fall monotonically across the three tiers. The shipped campaign is 40 / 30 / 25 percent today, monotone and discriminating; at the derived ranges it is 55 / 47 / 43, still monotone but compressed. That needs a derivation and a decision entry of its own, not a constant fitted to today's data — which is exactly why PRESSURE_FLOOR was deleted in decision 067.

Deliberately not done

  • The patch is printed and never applied. It lands with the first 48² content, per decision 082. Nothing under data/ changed in this commit; the diff is one new tool, the decision, and the backlog.
  • No dynamic coverage pass on the generated board. sim/coverage.py's InstrumentedSim is 6.8× overhead at 78 emplacements and one pass is about 21 minutes (LF-230). The geometric solve is the derivation; --grade is the check that was run instead.
  • LF-234's act-1 presence ceilings are not re-measured against the derived ladder — anchor-02 68.3%, 03 65.3%, 04 50.1%, 05 41.8%. Decision 082 puts that with the content rather than here.

Gate: tier 2, 26 passed, 0 failed, 16 correctly skipped, 32,372 ms. No rules file and no data file changed, so tier 2 is the bar. python syntax went from 63 files to 64 — the new tool tracked before the run rather than after it, which is the trap where a new untracked file is invisible to the gate, avoided on purpose this time.
