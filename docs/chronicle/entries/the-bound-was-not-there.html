The bound the ticket asked for was looked for and is not there, so the claim was weakened until it needed no constant

BALdecision 086

LF-243, decision 086, and the first landable slice of BAL-04. The ticket asked for win share per difficulty with a bound on the top tier. Both campaigns were graded in full — the shipped one and the derived-range one decision 082 refused — and every shape statistic came back indistinguishable: pooled share falls strictly in both, the drop ratio is 0.405 against 0.411, the constant-hazard fit 1.143 against 1.121. Only the level differs, and bounding a level needs a number fitted to today's data. So there is no bound in what shipped. Three candidate metrics were measured and rejected, and one of them turned out to be backwards.

This is the first piece of BAL-04, the campaign re-grade, and it deliberately is not any re-grading. BAL-04's headline acceptance criterion is that all 24 anchors grade ok at all three difficulties — and by the time this session opened, the grade table had been proved twice over not to be a valid selector for anything. Decision 081 measured board quality and grade count as anti-correlated across five candidate lattice orderings, and withdrew "every anchor still grades ok" as a criterion. Decision 082 then graded all 24 shipped anchors at the 48-square derived ranges and got 24/24 ok, unchanged, while the top difficulty's share of tried builds that win went 25% to 43%. Re-grading first would have produced a green table that means nothing. So the instrument came first.

The entry above this one, the-grade-table-said-nothing, is where that second proof was written down. This is what was done about it.

What was asked for, and what is not there

LF-243 asked for a metric — win share per difficulty, distinct_winning_builds / distinct_builds_tried — that falls monotonically across the tiers and holds the top tier under a bound. The falling half was easy. The bound was looked for, over both campaigns graded in full at 1,440 runs each keeping every per-policy outcome, and it is not there to be found. Every statistic about the shape of the difficulty curve is indistinguishable between the shipped campaign and the one that dissolved:

Monotonicity, spacing and curvature all pass on the campaign that dissolved. Only the level differs. Which means any test that catches it has to bound a level — and a bound on "how forgiving is too forgiving" is a number fitted to today's data, which is precisely the thing decision 067 deleted PRESSURE_FLOOR for rather than tune. Hunting for the constant that separates 24.2% from 42.6% would have been fitting to the sample, dressed up as a rule.

The claim that survived

The top difficulty's win share must fall strictly below the bottom one's, on every anchor. Nothing else.decision 086

That is the weakest statement about difficulty that is not vacuous, and it contains no number at all. It is also strictly stronger than the rule it joins: the old guard fired only when a tier's win share hit exactly 1.0 — every distinct build tried clears it — which is a knife edge silent one build short of itself, and is how a change that roughly doubled how forgiving the top tier is passed the campaign's own instrument without moving a single verdict.

sim/run.py's verdict() was extracted as a pure function of the grade table, with no Sim and no content behind it, for a reason worth stating plainly: three of its four rules cannot fire on any anchor that ships. A gate that grades the shipped 24 and asserts they pass therefore only ever exercises the green path of each one. --selftest drives 12 synthetic tables — 6 that must produce a problem, 4 that must stay silent, 2 structural — and tools/check.py runs it as grade verdict at tier 1 in about 220 ms, with no Godot and no content load. It was proved red four ways by breaking behaviour rather than an expectation: comparison inverted, rule deleted, the tutorial exemption widened to every anchor, and the top tier mis-read as the middle one. Same argument as firing arcs agree and verbs agree, one level up.

Does it actually catch anything

Yes, and this is where a number in the commit body needs correcting. 0 of 24 shipped anchors fail. 3 of 24 fail at the derived ranges — anchor-02, anchor-07 and anchor-23. A fourth anchor, anchor-01, trips the same rule at the derived ranges (3 of 5 builds win on standard, 3 of 5 on brutal) and is relaxed because it is declared a tutorial in data, so it reports ok. The commit message and decision 086 both compress that to "4 of 24 fail … and anchor-01, which is exempt as a tutorial", which does not sit with the tool's own summary line of 21/24 clean. An exempt anchor does not fail. Recording the discrepancy here rather than editing either document, because both are append-only.

The exemption carries a trap worth writing down for whoever adds the next rule. It filters the anchor's problems list by substring, so a new rule whose message happens not to contain a matching phrase is silently not relaxed on anchor-01 — and anchor-01 is the one anchor where relaxation is load-bearing, with five distinct builds tried against thirteen to sixteen everywhere else, which quantises its win share into 20-point steps.

Three metrics measured and rejected

All three separate the two campaigns. All three were thrown away, with their numbers, in decision 086 — and the rejections are the more useful record.

The second of those has a consequence beyond this change. BAL-04's own acceptance criteria make the same misreading, from the opposite side: its second bullet asks that every anchor carry at least 8 distinct winning builds, citing decision 044. One reading turns the number into a ceiling, the other into a floor, and the shared error is treating a scorer's saturation point as a bound on a tier at all. The shipped campaign does not meet the floor and never has — anchor-01, 02, 03, 04 and 08 are all under it on the sum — so BAL-04 would be graded against a criterion its own baseline fails. That bullet needs rewriting before the re-grade runs against it, which is LF-255. Worth being exact about the scope: two of BAL-04's eight acceptance criteria are phrased in terms of the grade table, not all of them. The other six are capacity saturation, rebased wave density, PRESSURE_FLOOR, dialog capacity, wave composition and rules parity, and none of them is touched here.

The one that came back backwards

The third candidate was the session's first hypothesis and by far the most appealing, because it needed no constant of its own. sim/engine.py's DIFFICULTIES table carries a comment saying the top tier's 1.55 multiplier was swept to be "the point where brutal kills the overdraw build but leaves the disciplined one alive". If that were true, the gap between the win rates of disciplined boards and overdrawing ones should widen as difficulty rises — a parameter-free test, handed over by the code itself.

Measured over all 1,440 runs of the shipped campaign, splitting on whether a run ever browned out: runs that brown out win far more often than clean ones.

standardhardbrutal
clean runs win31.4%23.5%17.9%
runs that brown out win53.2%41.2%33.5%
gap−21.8 pts−17.6 pts−15.6 pts

Overdrawing the reactor bus is worth roughly twenty points of win rate at every difficulty, and the advantage narrows as difficulty rises rather than widening. It is not one greedy policy dominating the sample — 171 to 173 runs per difficulty brown out, out of twenty policies, so many of them do it. The likely mechanism is that the overdraw penalty is priced against a board that is otherwise the same, while an overdrawing board has more guns on it, and more guns at reduced output beats fewer guns at full output.

That is a real finding about the currency this whole game is built on, and it is filed as LF-253 rather than acted on here — nothing in what shipped depends on the answer, because the metric it came from was rejected. It belongs to BAL-04 and should be settled before the capacity rebase, since a capacity ladder tuned on top of an inverted incentive tunes the wrong thing. One more wrinkle: at the derived ranges the trend inverts again, the gap going −11.9 to −22.2 and widening, so range interacts with it.

What was deliberately not done

Only the top tier is asserted. The middle tiers are reported and not tested, and the reason is measured rather than cautious: hard does not fall below standard on anchor-08 (3 of 14 builds win on both) or anchor-15 (6 of 13 on both) today. Both are real "hard is not biting here" findings — on those two anchors the tier's 1.35× enemy health and 0.90× bounty buy nothing, the same builds clear either way — and both are filed as LF-254. They are not asserted because a check that is red on arrival gets disabled rather than fixed, which this project has already watched happen (LF-224). Instead both anchors are named on every full grade and every wrap, in the footer above, so it cannot go quiet.

The thinnest passing anchor is worth naming while the margin is known. anchor-21 grades 4 winning builds of 15 tried on standard and 4 of 16 on brutal — the same absolute count, passing only because brutal happened to try one more build, on a win-share margin of +1.7 points against a campaign median around +11. The count-based form of the same rule fails on anchor-21 and on no other shipped anchor. LF-256 records it so that when BAL-04 loosens something and this check goes red there, it is a known thin margin and not a surprise — and so nobody weakens the rule to accommodate it.

The general lesson

When two candidate states of the world differ only in level and not in shape, no amount of ratio-taking will separate them, and the honest move is to weaken the claim until it needs no constant — not to keep hunting for the constant that happens to fit.decision 086

The commit records a tier-4 gate across the change: 43 passed, 0 failed, 447,360 ms, with both parity legs reporting a cached skip because no rule file and no data file moved, so the content digest is unchanged. A cached skip is not a pass, which is why the tally is 43 and not the registry's 45. The same commit records tools/sweep.py anchor-20 still returning 27 of 27 clean cells under the tightened rule. Neither of those two was re-run for this entry; the two listings above were, and the tier-1 grade verdict check re-measured at 220 ms.
