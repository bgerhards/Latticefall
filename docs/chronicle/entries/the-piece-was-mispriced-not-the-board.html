The brief was eight anchors; the defect was one row of data/towers.json

BALdecision 090

LF-254 and decision 090 — BAL-04's first content slice. The dispatch was "do an Act I balance pass", because three separate BAL-04 findings all pointed at the same eight anchors. They were not five anchors. They were one tower's stat: the arc node was paying a heavy weapon's power price for a light weapon's profile, 0.577 damage per megawatt against the pulse turret's 1.000 and 0.192 against flat armour 4, and it appeared in zero winning builds on all six Act I anchors where it is unlocked. Three numbers moved. Multi-weapon went 17/22 → 22/22 achievable anchors with zero per-anchor regressions, and no capacity_mw, wave table, lives or dialog line moved at all.

The brief dispatched this morning was do an Act I balance pass. Three separate BAL-04 findings pointed at the same eight anchors, so a balance-analyst went at the anchors: retune wave tables, capacity and lives across anchor-01 through anchor-08. Yesterday's the-criteria-were-never-measured (decision 088) had rebased BAL-04's acceptance criteria against measurement for the first time and found five achievable multi-weapon misses — anchor-03, 04, 05, 07, 08 — and called them, in its own words, "real content findings for this workstream rather than defects in the criterion".

They were not five anchors. They were one row of data/towers.json, and no anchor lever could have reached it.

Nine rows and one division

The measurement that found this was not a sweep over the anchors. It was dividing damage by megawatts for the nine rows of data/towers.json and noticing that one row was in the wrong band.

The arc node sat in the ion-lance power band and bought nothing for sitting there: no range — 3.8, the shortest in the game, against the turret's 4.0 — no splash, no shield targeting, and the worst armour penetration on the board. Against warden-heavy's flat armour 4 it fell to 0.192 damage per megawatt against the pulse turret's 0.556, because 6 damage a shot minus armour 4 is 2 net.

The grade table agreed without being asked. The arc node appeared in 0 winning builds on all six Act I anchors where it is unlocked, and in 54 instances campaign-wide against the pulse turret's 2,492. Its own note in the data file had been making a claim about it for the whole time:

Chains. Draw is high enough that two of them force a real decision.data/towers.json, arc-node.note, before this change

The decision it forced was to never build one.

Why no wave table could have fixed it

Act I capacity per emplacement slot is 9.2–11.0 MW against a 12 MW turret, so on every Act I board power binds strictly and slots never do. Under a strictly power-bound board, damage per megawatt is the only figure of merit — and a 1.73:1 ratio does not move for a wave table, a lives count or a slot layout. Five anchors were carrying a criterion failure for a tower they could not have fixed.

Derived, not swept

The draw was not searched for. The rule stated first: the arc node must stay strictly less power-efficient than the pulse turret against every enemy in the game, measured in kills per second per megawatt with overkill counted — integer shots to kill, not raw damage per second, because a 6-damage weapon wastes less on a 22 hp target than a 9-damage one. warden-mote has 22 hit points: four arc shots deliver 24, three pulse shots deliver 27. Solving that equality per enemy gives the draw at which the two weapons are exactly as efficient:

enemyarc-node draw at parity with pulse-turret
warden-mote16.88 MW — binding
warden-drone16.07 MW
hollow-drift15.75 MW
hollow-shard15.00 MW
reach-picket14.06 MW

The binding threshold is 16.88, so draw ≥ 17 — and every draw in data/towers.json is an even number of megawatts, so 18. The same derivation on the upgrade gives 25.0 and 22.5, so 26. Re-derived independently while writing this entry: at 18 MW the arc node is 0.833 damage per megawatt against the turret's 1.000 unarmoured, and 0.278 against 0.556 into flat armour 4 — still exactly half the turret against weight, which is the shape the rule was written to preserve. Decision 074's sentence about it drawing 2.2× for its chain is 1.5× now, and that decision is superseded by reference rather than edited.

The band was graded anyway, because a derivation that lands on a cliff edge is a guess with arithmetic in front of it. 15–18 MW is a plateau: 24/24 ok and zero regressions at all four values, multi-weapon 20–21 of 22. 19 MW and above regresses anchor-12 or anchor-10. The one-anchor wobble inside the plateau is quantisation noise — LF-264, filed in this pass — and is not claimed as signal.

One of the three numbers is not derived, and it says so

anchor-04 needed a second, separate number and it was money: starting_funds 620 → 590. The mechanism is derived. At 620 credits the rapid policy affords a third 100-credit turret before the 80-credit scan relay, reaching 90 of the anchor's 94 MW and leaving 4 MW — less than the relay's 8. So it plays the entire anchor blind to air and dies on wave 4. 94 MW is the only capacity in [88, 106] that leaves less than a relay after a weapon-first fill.

The value is not derived, and the analyst said so plainly instead of dressing it up. 584–596 is one verified plateau — every value ok at all three difficulties, carrying a multi-weapon winner, with hard biting, at standard+brutal 9–10 — and 590 is its midpoint. The campaign's own funds ramp (starting funds as a fraction of the reference board: 60.0 / 67.6 / 71.8 / 79.5 / 87.2 / 93.2 / 106.8 / 102.3% across Act I) puts anchor-04 exactly on the 03↔05 interpolation and does not pick 590. It is recorded that way here because how a number was reached is worth more than how tidy it looks, and a plateau midpoint honestly labelled is a better artefact than a derivation invented after the fact.

Robustness, with the weakest closure named

Nine perturbations per anchor — capacity ±2, drone ±1, mote ±1, heavy +1, lives −1. ok holds 7–9 of 9 on every Act I anchor. The multi-weapon result holds 9/9 on anchor-06, 7–8/9 on 03/04/07/08, and 4 of 9 on anchor-05. anchor-05 is the weakest closure in the pass and is flagged rather than rounded up into the 22/22 headline. The single perturbation that breaks it is heavy+1, every time — exactly what the armour arithmetic at the top of this entry predicts, since the heavy is the unit the arc node is worst against.

Refused: buying the closure with reactor capacity

Per-anchor capacity was the obvious lever and it was tried before it was refused, with numbers. It works on anchor-03: 104 MW takes standard+brutal from 6 to 9 and is robust to 8 of 8 perturbations. It works nowhere else. The monotone ladder then forces every later Act I anchor up, and anchor-07 cannot take it — 116 MW gives standard+brutal 9 with no multi-weapon build, while 112 and 124 give multi-weapon but flatten all three difficulty tiers to identical win shares and drop standard+brutal below its baseline of 7. anchor-04 at 112 and anchor-05 at 120 each lose their multi-weapon build under a ±1 change to a single spawn entry.

So the offer was +12 to +30 MW spread across six anchors to buy one robust closure and two knife edges. That is the trap CLAUDE.md names in a single line — density must never be paid for with reactor capacity — and this project has already sprung it once.

Refused: LF-218's rise in hit points per unit

Graded at three drone→heavy substitution depths on all six anchors, with unit counts falling so screen presence does not rise. Every depth that moved hit points per unit meaningfully took brutal to unwinnable or single-solution, or destroyed the multi-weapon result, on every anchor. The reason is the same armour arithmetic again: Act I's only unit with mass carries armour 4, which is flat, so raising the armoured share raises brutal difficulty superlinearly (hp ×1.55) and deletes the second weapon class at the same time. LF-218 needs a new Act I enemy — armour 0, roughly 90–130 hit points, ground — which is content, not a knob. Filed as LF-261.

Act I is a reveal gate, and that is why hard could not bite

The sharpest finding of the pass, and it was not what the pass went looking for. Across anchors 02–08, a build containing a scan relay won 136 of 246 runs. A build without one won 0 of 195 — dying on wave 3 or 4 at every difficulty, because warden-mote is kind: air and nothing else in the Act I roster can see it.

That is why hard was failing to bite on anchor-04 and anchor-08 in the first place: multiplying enemy hit points by 1.35 cannot move an outcome decided by whether a unit can be shot at all. The relay costs 8 MW of a 92–110 MW bus, so it is a tax rather than a trade — you do not choose it, you pay it. Whether that is the right shape for Act I is a design question, not a tuning one, so it is filed as LF-262 and left alone.

A bug that was not one, and a real one standing next to it

The analyst reported that the shield wall's upgrade makes the shield wall worse: base slow 0.28, upgraded slow 0.20, read as a downgrade. It is not a bug. slow is a speed multiplier — speed = kind.speed * slow — so lower is stronger, and the upgrade is a genuine improvement.

But checking the claim surfaced a real defect standing right next to it. _covered_by() takes max() over that value, and for a multiplier max() returns the weakest field covering the point — so an upgraded shield wall overlapping a base one has its stronger slow silently discarded. Both engines do the identical thing, so rules parity is perfectly clean and this is a shared design defect rather than a divergence: exactly the class of fault a parity harness is structurally blind to. Filed as LF-263.

This is the third session running in which briefing an agent to check its own brief, or checking the agent's report rather than filing it, caught something real. It is recorded here as method rather than as a scolding: the report was wrong about the bug, right that something was worth looking at, and the check cost about five minutes.

What the verification cost

rules parity had to run fresh on both platforms for a three-number data change, because the parity digest covers all 79 tracked data files and not just the two rule implementations — a tower's draw invalidates the cache exactly as a rules edit would. 1,512 runs identical on Linux (15 m 35 s, one run) and 1,512 identical on Windows across 8 shards, sharded because the full Windows leg does not fit the tooling's 10-minute command ceiling. 1,512 is 24 anchors × 21 policies × 3 difficulties — the 21st policy is yesterday's upgrade-ladder, from depth-is-bought-with-width.

Gate at tier 3: 42 passed, 0 failed, with anchor grades reporting 24 anchors ok at every difficulty. music loudness (tier 4) was not run, because no audio file changed. tools/parity_costs.json also moved in the commit and is not a content change — it is the Linux run's own per-anchor timing recalibration, written by the run that verified everything above.

Found while writing this entry, and not fixed here

The shield wall's note in data/towers.json still reads that it "ties the arc node as the biggest draw in Act I". After this change it does not tie — 26 MW against 18 — and it was not strictly true before either, since the ion lance unlocks at anchor-06 at 34 MW. It is a prose field with no validator behind it, which is why nothing went red. Recorded here because it is true and it is the kind of thing that rots quietly; not fixed in this change, because a chronicle entry does not get to edit the data it is reporting on.

The lesson, which is yesterday's from the other side

Decision 088 found that the acceptance criteria for the largest balance workstream had never been run against the campaign they describe. This one is the same shape one level down. Before tuning the level, check whether the piece placed on it is priced. The instrument that found a defect five anchors deep was one division over nine rows of a data file — cheaper than any sweep, and the sweep could not have found it at all, because the sweep only ever varied the anchors.

There is no screenshot on this page and that is deliberate. Three numbers changed in two data files and no pixel moved; a frame of the board would show the same board it showed yesterday, and an image taken to fill a slot is worth less than the sentence saying why there isn't one.
