  • August 1, 2026

    An instrument that reclassifies itself when you tune the thing it measures

    BAL

    LF-186, found during decision 074's range pass: sim/coverage.py's verdict() calls an anchor (a) geometry or (b) range by comparing its geometric ceiling against the ceiling at double the current range — and both terms scale with data/towers.json. Raising every weapon's range flipped four of five LF-181 geometry calls to range or adequately-sited while their dynamic p10 went flat or worse. The ceiling was never wrong; the label read as a claim about slots when it was a claim about slots-and-range together. Fixed by recording and printing the range basis a verdict was computed against, not by changing the measurement.

    The tail that would not move

    BALdecision 074

    The follow-up to the entry where the owner said most turrets could barely reach anything and the instrument agreed. LF-181 had concluded the dominant cause was range values, not slot placement. Decision 074 corrects that: raising range per weapon lifted the tail for arc-node and ion-lance but not for pulse-turret, which is 66–79% of every near-zero sample in the game.     The redundant sort is the contract: 24× and 34× off O(emplacements × units)

    WARdecision 072decision 058decision 027decision 030

    WAR-02 and WAR-03 close the tick-cost gate that WAR-06's unit budget and the still-open board-size call in PRD §6 both depend on. _step() was O(emplacements × units) with an inner O(units) splash loop — 24,000 distance checks a tick at 60 emplacements and 400 units at 30 Hz. WAR-02 resolves every unit's position once a tick instead of once per pair; WAR-03 adds a uniform grid sized larger than any real range or splash radius. Together: 20.08 ms to 0.84 ms at 512 units / 60 towers / 24 segments (24.1×), 58.43 ms to 1.73 ms at 1000/80/32 (33.8×), both PRD bars met. Selection was tested directly rather than inferred — decision 058 is the reason why — and decision 072 records the one thing about a spatial index that is invisible in its own diff: a sort that looks redundant because every bucket is already sorted, kept anyway, because it is the entire reason the indexed and exhaustive paths never disagree.

  • July 31, 2026

    Elevation is a blit offset, measured against the real camera

    TERdecision 017

    TER-01. E4's data foundation already existed — the anchor schema carries terrain regions and “terrain parsers agree” is a green gate check. What did not exist was the spatial half: a z on every drawable, so a tile at elevation actually draws higher than one at ground level. The constants come from the measured camera, not the nominal tile — 32.0 px per level, derived from k = s·cos(30°) and checked against two throwaway Blender renders to within 0.004%. Proved two independent ways on anchor-01's authored plateau: the engine's own reported positions, and the rendered pixels of the screenshot itself.

  • July 31, 2026

    It is the levels

    BALdecision 067

    The owner, playing: “the placement of most turrets are barely able to hit the enemies on the field.” Nothing in this project could see that — the sim only asks whether a build wins. sim/coverage.py (LF-176) is the instrument built to look, and it agrees with the owner: the median gun has a target in range about a third of the time, roughly one in five placed emplacements does essentially nothing, and the geometry says why — range values short relative to lane length on most anchors, slots simply too far from the lane on five Act 1 anchors. No range or slot was changed to get this answer.

    Measuring the fifth non-negotiable, for the first time

    PRC

    CLAUDE.md's fifth non-negotiable — loudness-match audio, never peak-normalize — had never been measured by anything in the gate. The only audio check, check_sfx_reproducible, verifies determinism, not loudness; a synthesis change that reproduces byte-identical but drifts the whole bank's perceived level would have passed every check the project has ever run.
