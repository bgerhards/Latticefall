The number that did not decide it

PRCdecision 073

The board size decision had been blocked for sessions on one missing number — a measured frame cost at scale. Decision 072 delivered it, 1.73 ms/tick at 1000 units and 80 towers, a 33.8x speedup. And then it decided nothing, because the sim fits at 32², 48² and 64² alike. What actually settled decision 073's 48² target was legibility and authoring, two things nobody had been waiting on.

Decision 073 takes PRD §6 open decision #2, the last genuinely-open owner call in that table: boards target 48 × 48. It also records that Docker stays — the standing systemd service from #83 is what builds and tests gate.yml's container image locally, and removing it would leave CI-image changes verifiable only by pushing and watching, which is exactly how decision 071's two red runs happened.

The interesting part is not the number 48. It is that the number everyone was waiting for turned out not to be the deciding one. The decision had sat blocked for sessions on “we need a measured frame cost at scale.” Decision 072 (WAR-03) delivered it — 1.73 ms/tick at 1000 units / 80 towers, a 33.8x speedup. And then it decided nothing, because the sim budget is 5.6 ms, not 16.7 — the speed control goes to 3x, so the rules run 90 ticks per second of wall clock. At 1.73 ms/tick that is 5.2 ms, which fits at 32², 48² and 64² alike. WAR-03 unblocked the decision by supplying evidence; it did not narrow the choice. What narrowed it was legibility and authoring — two things nobody was waiting on.

Provenance, stated rather than left implicit because it matters: only the 64² column is measured directly (LF-104, plus PRD §2.2's 13.4 ms/frame naive-draw figure against CAM-07's measured 2.47x improvement). The other two columns are that measurement scaled — zoom-to-fit is exactly linear in board edge, since a board of edge N projects to N × TILE_W px wide and the strip between the two instrument panels does not move. Arithmetic on a measurement, not a measurement.

Why each option lost

64² was rejected on the row the PRD's own break-order table already ranked second: sprite legibility, which that table puts at 48x48 as the breaking point. 64² renders a sprite at 30–60 px, and the rescue does not exist — raising Iso.TILE_W is blocked because calibrate() will not converge at 384 or 1024 px cells (LF-102 — 256 and 512 work by luck), and decision 066 had already refused atlas growth on a measured 630 MB VRAM projection. Choosing 64² would have meant choosing silhouette-first art as well, a far larger commitment than a board dimension.

32² was rejected despite being the only option needing no concession at all — legible, ~1.4 ms of draw, and the only size where the existing 24 anchors could be grown by hand instead of regenerated. It loses because 3.8x the area does not carry the programme it exists to serve: WAR-06's 250–400 alive units and WAR-01's 2–5 lanes with real standoff do not fit on it in a way that reads as a front line.

What it costs

  • Hand-authoring is dead at 48². LF-080's layout generator moves onto the critical path before any theatre-scale content exists, not alongside it.
  • It settles half of open decision #1 as a side effect, said out loud rather than discovered later: 48² needs zoom 0.312x to fit, so CAM-05 option 2 — a zoom floor with CAM-04's minimap doing the wide read — becomes the only affordable answer, and the board is never fully visible in the playfield. A design statement, made on purpose, consistent with decision 056. What remains open in #1 is only the art bar for E6.
  • Density work is unblocked — BAL-04's half now rebases against 2,304 tiles.

PRD §6's table listed five open owner decisions. All five are now taken — #1 half-settled by this one, #2 by decision 073 itself, #3 and #4 by decision 069, #5 by decision 068. The table is rewritten as a record of where each answer lives rather than a list of what is pending.
