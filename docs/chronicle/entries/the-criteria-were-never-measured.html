Three failures, or four

The old section had eight bullets, one of which is compound — a robustness floor and a mixed-weapon requirement in the same sentence — which is how eight bullets are nine criteria. The table above has eight rows because the ninth criterion, wave density with rebased per-act figures recorded in docs/STATE.md, is not a baseline that can pass or fail today: it passes as it stands (act 1 32 units on screen, act 2 27 at 85%, act 3 21 at 66%) and the rebase it asks for is this workstream's own output.

Which leaves a discrepancy worth recording, in an entry about counts that were never checked. The commit subject, decision 088's opening line, the rewritten section's preamble and LF-255's closing note all say four of nine bullets failed. The table each of them publishes enumerates three — the robustness floor, the mixed-weapon requirement, the composition bound — plus PRESSURE_FLOOR, which is the already-satisfied one. This journal is append-only and so is the decision log, so nothing is corrected there; it is written down here instead. Every underlying figure reproduces exactly, and they were all re-derived for this entry rather than taken from the commit body.

ROBUST_ENOUGH = 8, now read four ways

Decision 044 introduced the digit inside a scorer. tools/sweep.py's cell_score() computes it over standard_wins + brutal_wins and 044's own words are that build count "pays up to ROBUST_ENOUGH and stops" — a point of diminishing returns for a search, so that a sweep cannot buy a green cell with an endless supply of marginal builds. It is a saturation cap on a benefit. It has since been read as a bar four separate times:

  • 044, as written: a cap on the benefit of extra builds inside the sweep's scorer, over the two tiers summed.
  • BAL-04's acceptance bullet: a per-anchor floor over the same sum — under which 8 of the 24 anchors fail today.
  • docs/STATE.md's priority list: per anchor per tier. No anchor in the shipped campaign reaches 8 distinct winning builds on the top difficulty; the highest is anchor-14 at 7, so every anchor in the game fails that reading.
  • The candidate metric decision 086 rejected: a per-tier ceiling built on the same misreading, rejected there for the same reason.

Read as a per-anchor floor over standard + brutal, here is what the shipped campaign actually has on the eight anchors that miss it:

The bullet that could not be met

"At least one winning build on each anchor contains more than one weapon id" is impossible on anchor-01 and anchor-02, which unlock exactly one weapon between them. Five emplacements in data/towers.json deal damage — the pulse turret, the arc node, the ion lance, the flak array and the mortar — and the first of them unlocks at anchor-01, the second at anchor-03. What unlocks at anchor-02 is the scan relay, which does no damage and is therefore not a weapon id. Two anchors were permanently red against a criterion no content change could ever satisfy.

Conditioned on "two or more weapon ids unlocked", 17 of the 24 anchors meet it today. Seven miss; two of those seven are the impossible ones, so the achievable score is 17 of 22 and there are five real misses: anchor-03, 04 and 05 with two weapons unlocked, and anchor-07 and 08 with three — while anchor-06, on the same three, does meet it. All five are Act I. Those five stop being a defect in the criterion and become content findings for the workstream, which is a strictly more useful thing for a bar to produce.

The composition bound becomes a regression bound

"No act has more than 10% of its spawn entries at count: 1" was failed by all three acts on the day it was written:

actentries at count: 1spawn entriesshare
act 11514810.1%
act 210636129.4%
act 310832033.8%

The intent behind it is sound and is unchanged. It was written after a transform free to cut the wave table took 251 of 252 Act III spawn entries to count: 1 and reduced every wave to "N shards and one of each" — a content loss that no verdict in the grade table can see, because a flattened wave still grades fine. The repair keeps the intent exactly and drops the invented number: each act is pinned to its own measured share and forbidden to rise. Relaxing the bullet to a figure the acts pass — somewhere near 35% — was rejected, because it asserts nothing, while the regression form has no free parameter at all and is strictly stronger against the failure it was written for.

One check worth doing before trusting a baseline drawn out of the content: count is a required key in data/schema/anchor.schema.json under waves[].spawns[], and all 829 spawn entries in the campaign carry it explicitly. The share is a property of the authored content, not an artefact of a default in the counting script.

LF-257. Decision 086 recorded that at decision 082's derived ranges the power-discipline gap "inverts again and widens", −11.9 → −22.2 points. Decision 087 then showed the shipped version of that same statistic is an artefact of which policies overdraw. There is no range–power interaction in that number. The detail worth landing is in the top two rows: the uncontrolled statistic does not merely change magnitude between the two campaigns, it reverses its ordering across the difficulties — falling 21.8 → 15.6 in one and rising 11.9 → 22.2 in the other — while the controlled one stays within seven points of zero in both. That is the clearest single demonstration in the tree of why the raw split is not an instrument. It cost one script run against an already-cached grade, exactly as the backlog item predicted.

A note for anyone reading these three pages in sequence, because the arithmetic looks inconsistent otherwise: the backlog and decision 086 write these gaps negative (clean minus browned-out), and decision 088's table writes them positive (browned-out minus clean), which is the convention sim/run.py prints. Same magnitudes, opposite sign. The previous entry made the same note for the same reason.

LF-054 — PRESSURE_FLOOR — closed as already done. Decision 067 deleted it rather than raising it, because a threshold picked to make today's data fail is fitted to that data. Nothing under sim/ or tools/ computes it any more; only a tombstone comment remains, and that comment names the replacement worth building. The item survived as open for three sessions purely because BAL-04's task list and its acceptance criteria both still carried a bar for it. Both are corrected in this commit — a backlog item kept alive by two stale sentences in a spec nobody had re-read.

Two mistakes made on the way here, and they belong in the record

The first: LF-255, the backlog item that started this whole pass, was filed this same morning and named five anchors under ROBUST_ENOUGH when there are eight. It listed anchor-01, 02, 03, 04 and 08 with the right numbers and missed anchor-05, 07 and 23 entirely, because it was written from a partial scan and not re-derived before filing. That is an incomplete count inside an item whose entire point was that a number had not been checked against the thing it described. The closing note now carries the measured list and says which one it is.

The second was nearly a spectacular finding. The first attempt at the mixed-weapon baseline reported 0 of 24 anchors having a winning build with more than one weapon id — every anchor in the game, at every difficulty, apparently solved by a single emplacement type. It was wrong. A built entry in the grade JSON is not a tower id, it is "<tower-id>@<x>,<y>" with the position carried at four decimal places (pulse-turret@0.0000,4.0000, and free placement is why the coordinates are floats at all), so a filter matching bare tower ids matched nothing on every anchor and the aggregate came back a clean, uniform, entirely false zero. It was caught by printing the actual build lists instead of trusting the total — the same move that produced every real finding today.

The lesson, three times in one day

Run the criterion against the baseline the day you write it.decision 088

Decision 086: the grade table could not see a difficulty dissolve. Decision 087: the discipline gap measured a population, not an effect. Decision 088: the acceptance criteria were never checked against the thing they describe. Three instruments, three defects, one day — and every one of them surfaced by running the instrument over the real campaign and reading the output, not by reading the instrument's code. A bar nobody can clear, sitting in a spec, is worse than no bar: it gets quietly reinterpreted at the end by whoever is tired, which is precisely how one digit acquired four meanings.
