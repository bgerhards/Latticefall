An anchor has lanes, not a lane

Anchor JSON carried one path array, and both rule engines were built around a single point-lookup function, one path length, and a leak test of distance travelled against it. Two to five simultaneous lanes and up to four spawn points — the thing that makes a front line exist at all — needed that structure to move first, and it was the blocker behind fifteen other war-economy issues.

Decision 059: lanes are an index, never a name

path became paths — an array of id-and-waypoints objects — and every rule that touches a lane takes a required integer index into that array, never the string id. A default index would have let a missed call site compile and quietly assume lane zero, which is the one failure mode that could have survived every check in this repository: the game would run, parity would pass, and units on a second lane would silently walk the first lane's geometry. Ten internal call sites were updated to require the index explicitly, with no fallback.

The migration itself is proved, not asserted. A tool converts every existing single-lane anchor into a one-entry paths array with no lane key added anywhere, and a full detailed grade of all 24 anchors taken before the migration diffs empty against one taken after — 35,356 lines, zero differences. 'A single-lane anchor is the degenerate case of a multi-lane one' is now something measured rather than assumed true.

Decision 060: one yaw count, and a hysteresis fraction

The number of sprite facing directions was written independently in three separate places, and the constant that keeps a facing from flickering between two buckets — 12.0 degrees — was already larger than half a bucket at sixteen directions (11.25 degrees), which would have frozen every emplacement's facing permanently the moment that count was ever raised, with no visible sign of what had gone wrong. The constant became a fraction of a bucket instead of a fixed number of degrees, correct at any yaw count by construction rather than by coincidence. Measured on a real combat replay: at four directions, the new hysteresis cut facing reversals from 21 to 16 out of 84 total changes; at sixteen directions, from 90 reversals in 307 changes down to 59 in 263. No setting reached zero changes — which is the number that would have meant a frozen facing, the exact failure this exists to prevent.

Decision 062: furthest along means furthest along its own lane

The most consequential finding multi-lane paths exposed, and it was invisible until they existed. Targeting picked the furthest-advanced reachable unit by raw distance travelled along its own lane, compared directly across lanes with no normalisation at all. anchor-09's main lane is 37 tiles and its flank is 14 — so any gun that could see both lanes targeted the main lane almost exclusively from the moment a main-lane unit passed 14 tiles in, about eight seconds into every wave. The flank slots were never actually defending the flank; they only looked like they could on a chart of raw distance.

The fix compares progress as a fraction of each unit's own lane length rather than raw distance. The intuitive-sounding alternative — remaining distance to the exit — was checked and rejected on measurement, not on taste: it inverts the bug rather than fixing it, because the flank's remaining distance is capped at 14, so a live flank unit would outrank a main-lane unit anywhere across 62% of that lane, regardless of how far the main-lane unit had actually travelled. Single-lane boards are untouched by construction, since a fraction of a constant preserves order — proved across all 23 single-lane anchors, byte-identical across the full 24-anchor detailed grade.

Landing the fix left anchor-09 unwinnable at hard and brutal — from 9 of 16 winning builds down to zero, and 6 of 16 down to zero — because its acceptance case had deliberately been left light, to prove the plumbing rather than to be a finished level. It grades clean now at all three difficulties, and the fix was not the coverage split it first looked like: two slots were placed where a tower physically cannot see the main lane at all, which is what makes their targeting exclusively the flank's, and the flank's own wave timing was widened to match. Only anchor-09 moved in the full 1,152-run parity comparison, and no cell flipped a result — one policy still wins, at 3 lives instead of 4, with one more leak, which reads as attention genuinely split between two lanes rather than one lane being ignored entirely.
