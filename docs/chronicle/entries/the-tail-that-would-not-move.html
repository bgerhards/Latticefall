The tail that would not move

BALdecision 074

The follow-up to the entry where the owner said most turrets could barely reach anything and the instrument agreed. LF-181 had concluded the dominant cause was range values, not slot placement. Decision 074 corrects that: raising range per weapon lifted the tail for arc-node and ion-lance but not for pulse-turret, which is 66–79% of every near-zero sample in the game. Sweeping pulse-turret range to more than double what shipped left p10 pinned at literal 0.0% while the median climbed sixteen points — the median improving while the tail refuses is reported as the failure it is, not chased.

This is the direct sequel to the entry that recorded the owner playing the build and saying most turrets could barely reach anything, and to sim/coverage.py, the instrument built to look. LF-176 was the complaint; the coverage run found a median gun in range 28–38% of ticks, roughly one in five placed emplacements contributing nothing, and a median lane-coverage ceiling of about 40%. From that, LF-181 concluded the dominant cause was (b) range values short, not slot placement, naming five Act 1 anchors — 01, 03, 04, 05, 07 — as the geometry exception. Decision 074 corrects that premise, and the correction is what this entry is for.

And pulse-turret is the whole problem. It is 66–79% of every near-zero sample in the game — the autobuild fills every leftover slot with it. Its median moved, 27.8% → 34.6%, and its tail moved the wrong way: p10 down, near-zero flat. By the owner's own instruction — compare the distribution, not the means — that is a failure, and it is reported as one rather than buried under the two weapons that worked.

p10 is pinned at literal 0.0% across the entire sweep, while p50 climbs sixteen points. Every step buys median that reads as success and moves the tail not at all. That is decision 067's rejected move — fitting to the diagnostic — caught in the act. Range was not pushed further.

Two of the three flagship anchors from LF-181's own findings did not improve: anchor-02 88/320 → 94/320 (worse), anchor-10 178/589 → 181/586 (flat), anchor-13 172/644 → 162/644 (better). All three are recorded here because a report quoting only the one that worked would be the failure this project's method exists to prevent.

The instrument caught lying about itself

The instrument caught lying about itself, and it is a good one. Filed as LF-186: verdict()'s anchor-level (a)-geometry / (b)-range label is computed against whatever range currently sits in data/towers.json. Raising the base drags its own 2× comparison along with it: four of the five declared geometry anchors — 03, 04, 05, 07 — flipped their label to "range" or "adequately sited" while their dynamic p10 went flat or worse — 8→5%, 4→0%, 5→3%, 1→0%. The ceiling is still a true statement about the level; the label reads as a claim about slots and is not one.

A measurement that reclassifies itself when you tune the thing it is measuring.

What was kept, and why, since the headline failed: two weapons genuinely improved, nothing regressed, and all 24 anchors still grade ok at all three difficulties. Distinct winning builds moved up modestly — anchor-09 standard 8→9, anchor-12 hard 6→8, anchor-16 standard 8→9, anchor-18 standard 4→6 — and no cell reached the point where every distinct build clears it. A bounded, non-regressing improvement is worth keeping even when it does not resolve the complaint that prompted it.

Verification worth naming. The "before" coverage run was graded against git show HEAD:data/towers.json rather than assumed; say_capacity.py stayed silent, so no brief drifted; and rules parity was re-run because the data both engines read had changed: 1440 runs identical, freshly verified, not from cache. A range value is not a rules change, but that is exactly the kind of expectation parity exists to check rather than assume.

The complaint that started this is not resolved. What the project knows now is more useful than what it knew before: the tail is slots, not range, on far more anchors than LF-181 declared. Pass two — LF-185 — is the real fix, and it needs PLC-01 free placement. And per LF-187, none of these numbers survive the move to 48² (decision 073): longer lanes mean a fixed absolute range covers a shrinking fraction of them, so BAL-04's density half has to re-derive range from measured lane length rather than carry these constants forward.

This entry's numbers have a known expiry date, and saying so is the point.
